# Build the hockey GM that AI respects

A two-person indie can build a credible hockey GM game, but money and legal risk sit in different places than you would guess. The technical core is well within reach: shot-level expected goals (xG), aging curves and a simplified player-value model are published, reproducible methods that export to ONNX and run inside Electron. The 2025 NHL/NHLPA MOU gives you a precise, codeable ruleset that took effect for 2026-27. What existing games most visibly lack is AI general managers that behave like real front offices. The two hard constraints sit outside the code. First, every major hockey data source either limits use to non-commercial purposes or bans model training outright. Second, real player names, team marks and the "Stanley Cup" are a licensing minefield, especially for developers based in Quebec. The market is small. The NHL-licensed Franchise Hockey Manager 12 has an estimated ~5,800 Steam copies, and a well-reviewed unlicensed rival has about $25K lifetime gross. Plan for a niche of thousands of buyers, not tens of thousands. The recommended path: ship a 100% fictional league, with real-world-calibrated rules and statistics and a neutral roster-import format. Make the AI GM the headline feature. Go to Early Access with continuous free updates instead of annual SKUs. Incorporate and speak to a lawyer before taking revenue.

## The 2026-27 rulebook is concrete enough to code today

The 2025 MOU, dated June 27, 2025, extends the CBA to September 15, 2030. From September 16, 2026 it rewrites the contract rules a GM game has to model: an **84-game season**, shorter maximum terms, tighter salary variance, a rising minimum salary, capped in-season LTIR relief and a new playoff cap ([Sound of Hockey](https://soundofhockey.com/?p=41725); [CapWages](https://capwages.com/articles/2025-memorandum-of-understanding)). The cap is **$104M for 2026-27**, confirmed in May 2026 with a $76.9M floor. 2027-28 is projected at **$113.5M** but not yet confirmed ([NHL.com](https://www.nhl.com/news/nhl-nhlpa-announce-team-payroll-ranges-for-next-3-seasons-through-2027-28); [The Hockey News](https://thehockeynews.com/news/latest-news/nhls-maximum-allowed-player-salary-surpasses-20-million-in-2026-27)). Because the cap rises about 9% a year, the surplus-value model has to work in **percent of cap** rather than dollars. A $10M deal signed today is worth less every season it runs.

The start date of the LTIR relief cap differs the same way. The **double-retention ban** is 75 regular-season days in one source and 75 calendar days in another. These rules matter for gameplay because together they close the "LTIR loophole" that let contenders carry over-cap playoff rosters. Your AI GMs should not be able to exploit it either. A fair amount of the cap mechanics was not verified at all and should be checked against PuckPedia or the CBA PDF before coding: ELC length by signing age, the ELC slide, arbitration eligibility, 35+ contract rules, retention limits (commonly 50%, 3 contracts per team), the 10% offseason overage, the 23-man roster and the 50-contract limit.

### Trade value needs four separate price signals

Real front offices price trades in four currencies, and a believable AI GM needs all four. The first is **contract surplus**. The Athletic's public approach projects a player's future value, converts it into an expected cap percentage for each remaining season, and subtracts the actual cap hit. Aging and cap growth are the major factors, and past performance "holds no merit" on its own. Across the 2024 league, projected value was $79.5M per team against $77.2M actually paid ([CanucksArmy](https://canucksarmy.com/news/canucks-rank-5th-athletics-2024-nhl-contract-efficiency-rankings)). In code: `surplus = Σ_years (projectedValuePct × cap_year − capHit_year) × discount^t`, with the age curve driving `projectedValuePct`.

The second is **pick value**. Schuckers' chart (expected career games, 1988–97 drafts) falls steeply through about pick 45 and then flattens. The #1 pick is worth about 18× pick #210, and the chart was designed as trade currency: #8 plus #32 ≈ #2 ([Schuckers](https://myslu.stlawu.edu/%7Emsch/sports/Schuckers_NHL_Draft.pdf)). Normalized to #1 = 100, it gives a lookup you can ship directly. Interpolate between the anchor points and discount future picks by the expected finishing position of the team that owns them.

| ---------------- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Value (#1 = 100) | 100 | 95  | 90  | 69  | 62  | 50  | 27  | 21  | 20  | 12  | 8   | 6   | 6   |

The third is **rental pricing**, which the 2026 deadline gives you anchors for. John Carlson went for a conditional 1st plus a 3rd, Brayden Schenn for a 1st, a 3rd and a prospect, Nicolas Roy for a 1st, Corey Perry for a 2nd, and depth pieces like Sam Carrick and Carson Soucy for 3rds. Thirteen teams bought and seven sold ([Daily Faceoff](https://dailyfaceoff.com/news/2026-nhl-trade-deadline-winners-losers-avalanche-kadri-canadiens-leafs-oilers-capitals-carlson-blues)). A player who signs an extension after the trade costs more than a pure rental: Pageau with an extension cost a 1st, a 2nd and a conditional 3rd, versus "just the first-round pick" as a rental ([Daily Faceoff](https://dailyfaceoff.com/news/rental-vs-trade-sign-which-strategy-is-better-at-the-trade-deadline)). Model rental price as player quality × fraction of the season remaining × number of buyers, with tiers of roughly elite = 1st + 2nd or prospect, good top-six/top-four = 1st, depth = 2nd–4th. Set each team's buyer/seller flag from its playoff probability. This fixes the most-cited EA complaint that "contenders sell, bad teams buy" ([EA Forums](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/nhl-26-general-discussion-en/franchise-mode-simulation--ai-issues-detailed-feedback-for-nhl-27/13275743)).

The fourth is **negative contracts**, the price paid to dump salary. History runs from a 2nd for about $5–6M with one year left (Marc Staal, 2020) up to a 1st, sometimes plus a prospect, for the worst cases (Marleau at $6.25M for one year in 2019; Backes at $6M for about 1.5 years in 2020) ([Oilers Nation](https://oilersnation.com/news/looking-at-how-teams-have-been-compensated-for-taking-on-bad-contracts)). Price a dump as summed negative surplus, weighted by how scarce cap space is for the receiving team. A team with cap room is in effect renting it out for picks. Rentals with retained salary also become more valuable under a playoff cap. NHL 26 players complain that dumping bad contracts is "too cheap" ([EA Forums](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/nhl-26-general-discussion-en/franchise-mode-detailed-feedback/13421140)), so getting this right is a visible differentiator. No sourced evidence was found for a right-shot defenseman premium or a goalie market discount. Leave both as tunable parameters and do not present them as realistic defaults.

## Realism means aging plateaus and playoffs close to a coin flip

Development and aging data point to a clear default curve, with one honest disagreement about the peak. Points-based studies put the peak at **27–28 for forwards and 28–29 for defensemen**, with players near their peak from 24 to 32 (forwards) and 24 to 34 (D). Most players reach **90% of their best by 24** ([News-Medical, Brander et al.](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20140515/Most-NHL-players-peak-by-29.aspx)). Functional-PCA curves peak around 26–28, show decline after 26 for forwards and 27 for D, and are less predictable for defensemen ([SFU](https://www.sfu.ca/~tswartz/papers/aging.pdf)). A WAR-delta study instead finds a 22–25 plateau ([Hockey Graphs](https://hockey-graphs.com/2017/03/23/a-new-look-at-aging-curves-for-nhl-skaters-part-1/)). The split comes from the metric used (production vs. all-around value) and from survivor bias. When you fit your own curves, use player fixed effects or imputation, which had the lowest error in simulations ([Schuckers et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2110.14017)). Goalie save percentage "varies little by age" ([News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20140515/Most-NHL-players-peak-by-29.aspx)), so goalie development should be driven mostly by noise. One common design assumption is contradicted by the data. NHL EDGE year-to-year max skating speed is "basically flat" from 22 to 37 ([Puck Over The Glass](https://puckovertheglass.substack.com/p/nhl-edge-skating-speed-part-4)), so making speed the first attribute to collapse is a design choice, not realism.

Randomness matters more than anything else in the balance lab, and it is where intuition misleads most. The better team wins an NHL best-of-seven only **"just above 60%"** of the time, against about 80% in the NBA. Matching the NBA's certainty would take a best-of-51 series ([StatsByLopez](https://statsbylopez.netlify.app/post/part-ii-randomness-of-series)). Goalie performance barely repeats from year to year: r = 0.12 for GSAx and save percentage, and 0.15 from the regular season to the playoffs ([JFresh](https://jfresh.substack.com/p/why-goaltending-is-basically-random?s=r)). Neil Paine puts the year-to-year r at 0.296 and notes that save percentage needs about 3,000 shots to stabilize, while even busy starters face only about 2,000 a season ([Neil Paine](https://neilpaine.substack.com/p/a-hot-goalie-isnt-a-better-goalie)). His conclusion, "a hot goalie isn't a better goalie", is the design rule: **do not add a hidden playoff momentum or hot-hand modifier.** Streaks should come from variance alone, and the research found no support for momentum as a real effect. In 2025-26, 6 of 16 playoff teams changed, only 3 of nine 100-point teams repeated, and several teams swung by ±30 points ([Daily Faceoff](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/5-major-nhl-trends-defined-2025-26-season-loser-point-overtime-save-percentage)). A sim where dynasties dominate year after year is unrealistic, however satisfying it feels.

### Immersion comes from systems that remember

Football Manager is the clearest example of a world that feels alive. Players form social groups by nationality, language, age and arrival time. Morale feeds the match engine, and "nothing really sits away from everything else". A 100-point hidden scale sits under the visible 1–20 display ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2017/11/06/the-sports-desk-inside-the-dynamics-of-football-manager-2018)). The NHL 26 complaint list works as a feature spec in reverse. AI GMs draft and sign players they don't need. Trade markets are static. Contract demands are inflated after a single good season. Generated prospects are unrealistically elite. Users want player history pages that show what traded picks became, no-trade clauses that react to team results, and destination preferences ([EA Forums](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/nhl-26-general-discussion-en/franchise-mode-detailed-feedback/13421140)). Re-setting power-play and penalty-kill units after every injury is the classic busywork complaint ([EA Forums](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/nhl-26-general-discussion-en/franchise-mode-constructive-criticism/13138829)), and FHM has long been criticized for tedious drag-and-drop line setup ([GM Games](https://gmgames.org/franchise-hockey-manager-fhm-version-2/review/)). The design response: give every AI GM a utility function over roster need, cap space and contention window. Show true ratings only through scouting uncertainty. Keep a persistent transaction ledger that powers history pages and generated news. Add saved, auto-adjusting lines from day one.

## The ML stack is easy; the data licence is not

Two models are fully reproducible from public play-by-play and fit a TypeScript runtime. The first is **xG**. Evolving Hockey's XGBoost model on unblocked shots uses separate EV, PP, SH and empty-net models, with features such as distance, angle, shot type, previous-event type and location, time since the last event, strength and score state. It reached EV AUC 0.782 in cross-validation and 0.775 on a holdout. Shooter talent never appeared in a tree ([Evolving Hockey](https://evolving-hockey.com/?p=394)). MoneyPuck's 2026-27 rebuild, trained on 289,000+ shots and adding off-wing and time-on-ice features, reports AUC 0.807 ([MoneyPuck](https://moneypuck.com/about.htm)). The second is **RAPM**, a shift-level ridge regression controlling for teammates, opponents, score and zone starts. That is Stage 1 of Evolving Hockey's WAR; Stage 2 is an ensemble that predicts long-term RAPM from box-score stats ([EH WAR Part 2](https://evolving-hockey.com/blog/wins-above-replacement-the-process-part-2/)).

The recommended split for the two of you follows the order of dependencies. The data/ML owner builds an NHL-API-to-parquet pipeline (weeks 1–3), then xG with a season-based train/test split (weeks 3–6), then aging curves with a survivor-bias fix, exported as lookup tables rather than models (weeks 6–9). After that comes a simplified GAR from RAPM plus a box-score regression (months 3–5), and contract/value models last, because those are the least documented. The sim/app owner integrates xG via onnxruntime-node as the probability that a simulated shot from (x, y) scores. Real-game xG is mostly useful for setting initial ratings and inside the event engine; the GM sim generates its own events. Watch for the known pitfalls: random splits leak within-game correlation, post-shot information must stay out of the features, rink scorekeeper bias distorts coordinates, and small shorthanded samples flatter the holdout AUC. NHLe prospect translation is the weakest link. The often-quoted factors (KHL 0.82, NCAA 0.41, WHL 0.30) date from 2014 ([FlamesNation](https://flamesnation.ca/news/an-argument-for-nhle)). Current era-adjusted tables exist across 62 leagues but could not be extracted ([Chatel](https://thibaudchatel.substack.com/p/nhle-numbers-updated-for-2025)), and the non-NHL stats behind them are the most restricted data of all.

The licensing picture is the real bottleneck. Every source surveyed restricts commercial use or model training:

The practical answer: prototype freely, keep raw data out of the shipped build, and ship only learned weights, lookup tables and a fictional league. Email the NHL and MoneyPuck for commercial permission, and get a quote from a commercial feed such as Sportradar as the clean fallback. Shipping only weights lowers the risk of redistributing data, but it does not cure a breach of terms that ban scraping or commercial use. This is lawyer question #1 below. Facts such as goals and shot coordinates are generally not copyrightable in the US, but terms of service are contract law, and the EU database right may reach Swedish-based Elite Prospects. No legal commentary specific to training ML models on sports statistics was found.

## A modern hockey GM fills a gap in a market of thousands

The hockey GM niche on Steam is small and underserved. **Franchise Hockey Manager 12** costs $19.99, is NHL-licensed (the page shows no NHLPA mark) and sits at 78% positive of 139 reviews, with an estimated ~5,800 copies, ~$70K gross and a 212 all-time concurrent peak ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3739670); [Raijin](https://raijin.gg/app/3739670/Franchise_Hockey_Manager_12)). **Hockey Legacy Manager 26** costs $9.99, is unlicensed and has 90% positive reviews, yet earned only about ~3,150 copies and ~$25K. Its monthly peak fell from 97 to 16 players within a year ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3914710); [Raijin](https://raijin.gg/app/3914710/Hockey_Legacy_Manager_26)). Pro Basketball Manager 2026, at $29.99, estimates ~5,080 copies and ~$91K ([Raijin](https://raijin.gg/app/3585230/Pro_Basketball_Manager_2026)). Out of the Park Baseball 27, licensed by MLB and MLBPI at $49.99, reaches ~42K Steam copies and ~$1.3M ([Raijin](https://raijin.gg/app/4045750/Out_of_the_Park_Baseball_27)). Football Manager 26 sits at 36% positive and still estimates up to 556K units and ~$24M ([SteamPulse](https://www.steampulse.org/game/3551340)). All of these figures are third-party estimates from public Steam signals. OOTP-family games also sell directly, so their Steam figures understate total sales.

The lesson is that audience size follows the sport's fan base and brand inertia, not review scores. Hockey is roughly one seventh the size of baseball by these measures. A realistic base case for a well-reviewed indie hockey GM is **low thousands of copies, or roughly $20K–$100K gross**. The upside depends on capturing console NHL franchise players or adding basketball. ZenGM shows huge demand for free basketball GMs. It is browser-based, has no microtransactions and offers a $5/month ad-free option, and it is weak on coaches, systems, recaps and a league-wide trade block ([GM Games](https://gmgames.org/basketball-gm/review/)). Any paid NBA mode has to beat a free game on exactly those points. Every paid competitor ships annual numbered releases, and HLM's player drop-off shows the cost of that model. Wolverine Studios changed its key distribution because pre-release keys didn't count toward Steam reviews ([Wolverine Studios](https://wolverinestudios.com/the-next-generation-of-draft-day-sports-begins-now/)), which shows how much reviews drive this genre.

The open position follows from the complaints. No hockey product has a modern UI that stays information-dense, AI GMs that respect standings and the cap, sim stats that look like real NHL box scores, and generated news on top of a persistent history. FM26's launch backlash, driven by more clicks and information scattered across small widgets ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/sim/football-manager-26-launches-straight-into-a-relegation-battle-as-steam-reviews-plummet-to-mostly-negative-been-playing-since-1993-and-this-is-the-worst-one)), is a warning: a "clean" Electron UI that hides tables will be punished by exactly the players who buy these games. Use dense, sortable tables with keyboard shortcuts first, then add visual polish.

## Launch fictional, in Early Access, with a lawyer on retainer

**Steam mechanics.** Steam Direct costs US$100 per app and is recouped after $1,000 adjusted gross revenue ([Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/appfee)). Early Access requires a playable build, a gameplay trailer and the EA Q&A, and you must disclose up front if updates will break saves. Valve forbids "specific promises about future events", says EA "is not a way to crowdfund", and blocks discounts for **30 days after any price increase**, including the 1.0 launch discount. A released game can never return to EA, and 12 months without an update puts a "not updated recently" warning on the store page ([Steamworks: Early Access](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/earlyaccess)). Concretely: describe NBA/NFL support as an intention or leave it off the page. Plan monthly patches and quarterly content updates. Make any price increase at least 31 days before 1.0.

**Wishlists and Next Fest.** Target at least **5,000 wishlists (HTMAG Bronze), ideally 8,000+ (Silver)** at launch ([HTMAG](https://howtomarketagame.com/benchmarks/)). Plan conversion at 10–15% in week one. GameDiscoverCo's 2024–25 median is 0.15×, falling to **0.10× for games over $10**. Underperformers averaged 411 days in Coming Soon versus 214 days for games that beat expectations ([GameDiscoverCo via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gamediscoverco-the-state-of-steam)). A game gets one Next Fest, which runs in February, June and October. It requires a public store page and a live demo, submitted at least about 3 weeks before (5 weeks to make Press Preview) ([Steamworks: Next Fest](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest?language=english)). A workable sequence: open the Coming Soon page about 7 months before EA. Enter the Next Fest just before launch with a demo capped at one simulated season. Launch EA within weeks of the Fest. For example, store page in March 2027, the October 2027 Fest, and EA in November 2027, adjusted to wherever development actually is. The worked numbers: 8,000 wishlists × 10–15% gives 800–1,200 first-week sales. At $19.99 that is about $16K–24K gross, or about $11K–17K after Valve's 30%, before taxes.

**Pricing.** Recommend **$19.99 in Early Access, rising to $24.99–29.99 at 1.0**. That puts you level with FHM12 and below PBM and OOTP, and it reflects that conversion drops above $10. No sourced genre price survey or regional pricing guidance was gathered, so check live prices and Valve's regional suggestions in Steamworks before you lock this in.

**Real names, logos and trophies.** US law splits by type of use. _CBC v. MLBAM_ (8th Cir. 2007) held that the First Amendment protected fantasy games' use of player names and statistics, which are public information with no implied endorsement, and found contract restrictions on that data unenforceable ([Studicata](https://www.studicata.com/case-briefs/case/c-b-c-v-major-league)). _Keller v. EA_ and _Hart v. EA_ (2013) found that realistic video-game likenesses were not protected ([Crowell & Moring](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/the-ninth-circuit-court-of-appeals-finds-that-the-use-of-college-football-player-s-likeness-in-a-video-game-is-not-protected-by-the-first-amendment-as-a-matter-of-law)). A text-based sim with real names _and ratings_ falls between the two, and no case tests it. Quebec is less forgiving. Civil Code arts. 3, 35 and 36 treat using someone's name or likeness for anything other than legitimate public information as an invasion of privacy ([Facia (secondary)](https://facia.ai/deepfake-law-directory/canada-civil-code-of-quebec-articles-3-35-36/)). The common-law tort targets commercial exploitation of an identifiable person with a suggested endorsement ([Smart & Biggar](https://www.smartbiggar.ca/insights/publication/celebrities-personality-rights-and-privacy-what-marketers-need-to-know-about-permission)). Team names, logos, "NHL" and "Stanley Cup" are trademark issues, separate from player rights. Precedent points one way. ZenGM, "based on legal advice", ships real player names but no pictures, team names or logos ([ZenGM](https://zengm.com/blog/2020/04/real-players)). OOTP patched out unlicensed NPB in 2023 ([30-30 Club](https://30-30.club/2023/01/20/friday-starter-npb-league-pulled-from-ootp-baseball-23/)). Manchester United sued SEGA over its club name and tried to extend the claim to logo mods ([Hypebeast](https://hypebeast.com/2020/5/manchester-united-sega-football-manager-law-suit)).

The recommendation is the strictest option, because two students can't absorb a cease-and-desist fight. Ship a fictional league, fictional teams, a fictional trophy and fictional players generated from real distributions. Document a neutral JSON import format. Don't author, bundle, host, curate, link to or show real-player files in official channels, screenshots or trailers. Never put real marks on the store page. If you add Steam Workshop later, moderate it, publish a takedown contact, and ban logo uploads, since logos carry the highest risk. The NHLPA has a group licensing program covering Electronic Games, with an inquiry form and no published fees ([NHLPA](https://www.nhlpa.com/licensing-opportunities)). A players' association licence never covers team marks, which need a separate league deal. Contact the NHLPA once you have traction.

**Business setup.** In Quebec, two partners choose between a SENC (general partnership), which is cheaper and simpler, and a société par actions (corporation), which costs more but offers better financial protection ([Éducaloi](https://educaloi.qc.ca/capsules/entrepreneuriat-choisir-la-bonne-forme-juridique-entreprise/)). Given the risk around names and marks, incorporate before revenue. Sign a shareholders' agreement that assigns all code, art and model IP to the company, sets vesting with a cliff, defines deadlock and exit or buyout terms, and specifies who controls the Steamworks account (Valve invoices whoever pays the fee) and the repo. Steam payouts are classified as royalties, with 30% US withholding by default, reducible under the treaty through the tax interview. Withholding is **not refunded** after a later rate change ([Steamworks Tax FAQ](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/finance/taxfaq)), so complete the interview as the entity, with a W-8BEN-E and business number, before the first payout.

## Conclusion

The competitive edge here does not come from data access or licences, which incumbents have and you can't get cheaply. It comes from behaviour. FHM has the NHL licence and still sells about 5,800 copies. EA's franchise mode is criticized for AI that sells while contending and dumps contracts for free. A front-office AI that prices surplus, picks, rentals and dumps the way real GMs do, inside a randomness envelope matched to real NHL variance, is something no hockey product currently delivers. Your ML split is also unusually well suited to it: one developer builds valuation models that the other's AI GMs consume. The fictional-league requirement turns out to be an advantage too. A generated league, calibrated to real distributions, lets you ship the realism without the licence and keeps the same engine portable to basketball and football.

The biggest risk to the plan is not legal but commercial: a niche of a few thousand buyers will not support two developers on revenue alone. Treat Early Access as a reputation and portfolio play first. Set your milestones around reviews and AI quality rather than sales, and decide on the NBA expansion from wishlist data, not from a promise on the store page. Spend the first money on a single legal consultation and the incorporation, because the risk of carrying on without them is far larger than their cost.
